Titanium Dioxide Polymorphs

Below are the three main polymorphs of titanium dioxide (𝑇𝑖𝑂2). Experimentally observed lattice data was used to model each structure:1

Rutile: This unit cell contains 2 Ti atoms and 4 O atoms. With a density of ~4.25 g/cm³, rutile is the most dense and stable form of Titanium Dioxide which is often used in coating, cosmetics, paints, and plastic items due to its high refractive index and white color. 2


Brookite: This unit cell is a low symmetric structure that contains 8 Ti atoms and 16 O atoms. It is currently used for its photocatalytic nature of absorbing light in the ultraviolet range. Due to it being less stable than rutile, it is less commonly found in nature. nature.3

Anatase: This unit cell contains 4 Ti atoms and 8 O atoms. It is currently used for its photocatalytic nature of absorbing light in the ultraviolet range. It is slightly more stable than brookite and heating can cause it to transform into rutile.2

Lattice Structures

Body Centered Cubic (BCC): BCC lattice structure contains 8 corner atoms and 1 atom in the center of the unit cell for a total of 2 atoms/unit cell. Based on this, BCC has a packing efficiency of 68%. Common metals with this structure include Fe, W, & Mo.4

Face Centered Cubic (FCC): FCC lattice structure contains 8 corner atoms and 6 face atoms for a total of 4 atoms/unit cell. Based on this, FCC has a packing efficiency of 74%. Common metals with this structure include Cu, Ag, Au, Ni, Pd, Pt, Rh, & Ir.5

Hexagon Closed Pack (HCP): HCP lattice structure contains 12 corner atoms and 2 atoms in the center of the unit cell for a total of 2 atoms/unit cell. Based on this, HCP has a packing efficiency of 74%. Common metals with this structure include Co, Zn, Ti, & Ru.6
