Longshore Casual

October 25, 2026

Findings

  • September 2026 was the busiest month at LA and LB ports: 1.04 million TEU at Los Angeles, its most in 118 years, and 997,177 at Long Beach, its most in 115 years. 1,2 TEU stands for Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit, a standard cargo container.
  • September and October 2026 are the casual hall's busiest months since April 2022.
  • Total longshore jobs strongly track cargo (r = 0.87); casual jobs moderately do (r = 0.59).3
  • About 29 casual jobs move the casual board through a letter.
  • 2026–27 forecast: about 516 letters in November, 565 in December and 562 in January.
  • The forecast may be low. It assumes cargo about 4% above last winter, but if total jobs keep September and October's pace, 28% above last year, the three months would be nearer 610, 700 and 710 letters.

Casual work against cargo

Total longshore jobs strongly track monthly cargo (r = 0.87); casual jobs moderately do (r = 0.59), and month to month barely at all (r = 0.25)." Casual jobs only follow long run cargo trends.

Letters moved

The casual hall dispatches jobs by letters, and in slower months letters advanced for less jobs than in busier months due to less casuals seeking work.

In the last 16 months with R2 = 0.99 and an error of 0.27 letters per working shift, the hall operated at 33 jobs per letter.

Casual share and jobs per TEU

The ports have needed fewer jobs per TEU each year: 77 per 1,000 TEU in 2022, 72 in 2023, 69 in 2024, 67 in 2025 and 66 so far in 2026. March 2022 dispatched 143,873 total jobs, more than September 2026, on 11% less cargo.

Searching for a long run trend, PMA's annual reports go back further, in hours paid rather than jobs.4 Since 2008, the two ports have needed 8% less longshore hours per TEU, and the casual share has fallen from 5.6% to 3.0%.

The 2014–15 peak was caused by the contract fight: PMA records union slowdowns at the major ports from early November 2014 until the tentative agreement of February 20, 2015, with hours paid while ships waited and more to clear the backlog, on top of that year's chassis shortages, larger ships and reshuffled carrier alliances.

Monthly Forecast

  1. Total jobs are the month's TEU forecast times 66.2 jobs per 1,000 TEU, the mean of the last twelve complete months (range 63.1–69.0).
  2. The casual share of total jobs is assumed to remain constant from Sep and Oct.
  3. 33 jobs per letter is assumed.

Registered workers checked in

Jobs reach the casual hall only after the registered Class A and Class B workers who checked in at the longshore hall have been dispatched. Over the last two years an average of 1,015 Class A and 512 Class B workers checked in for each day's 1st shift.3
